# RIMAC Treadmill Waiting Experience — UX Research Case Study

A qualitative HCI research project from UCSD COGS 102C studying how students navigate treadmill availability at the RIMAC gym during peak hours — and designing lightweight interventions to reduce uncertainty and social friction.

**Team:** Xinyi Liu, Sihan Wang, Junyou Feng, Yiwen Huang (Group 15 — "AI of Byte")
**Course:** COGS 102C — Qualitative HCI Research Methods, UC San Diego
**Field site:** RIMAC treadmill area, peak hours 12:00–14:00 & 18:00–20:00

## The problem

During peak hours, students must make decisions about treadmill use without clear information: a 20-minute usage rule exists but is rarely followed or enforced, and there is no visible queue or availability system. Users don't know equipment availability, waiting conditions, or who is next — leading to uncertainty, social awkwardness, inefficient waiting, and inconsistent access.

## Research process

- **Contextual observation** (~7 hours across 3 peak-hour sessions): documented waiting behaviors — avoiding direct interaction, repeatedly walking around checking machines, guessing who is next, giving up when uncertainty gets too high.
- **User interviews**: semi-structured interviews surfacing pain points — unclear waiting order, unpredictable wait times, social discomfort when approaching others, lack of reliable information.
- **Qualitative analysis**: coded data in Condens; sequence/modality/semiotic analysis; actor & activity models; pattern–breakdown tables; spatial activity maps with minute-level episode narratives.
- **Design translation**: hand-drawn storyboards and experience models communicating three design directions.

## Design directions

1. **UC San Diego app integration** — check treadmill occupancy before heading to the gym.
2. **Entrance TV display** — real-time occupancy at the RIMAC entrance.
3. **Button lights + 20-minute countdown timer** — non-verbal occupancy signals supporting fair equipment sharing.
